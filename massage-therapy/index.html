The short answer

Massage therapy is hands-on work on the soft tissue: the muscles, tendons, and fascia that get tight, guarded, and sore. In our office it is done by licensed massage therapists and used alongside chiropractic care rather than on its own, because loosening the tissue around a joint tends to make the rest of the plan work better and last longer.

Massage gets sold as a luxury and as a cure-all, and it is neither. Here is the grounded version: what it actually is, where it fits with chiropractic care, who it suits, and what a session is like.

What is therapeutic massage?

It is focused work on the soft tissue, matched to what your body needs that day. That can mean broad, relaxing strokes to settle a guarded area, or slower, deeper work into a specific knot or band of tension. It is not the same as a spa massage aimed purely at relaxation, though it can feel just as good; the goal here is to change how the tissue moves.

Because our therapists work in the same office as the chiropractors, the massage is aimed at the same problem your care plan is aimed at. The two are coordinated rather than run in separate lanes.

Who is it for?

Massage tends to help when tight, overworked muscle is part of the picture, which it very often is.

  • Neck and shoulder tension that builds through a desk day.
  • Low back muscles that guard and tighten around an irritated joint.
  • Recovery work for active people and athletes between harder efforts.
  • Areas that stay tense and keep pulling a joint back out of its easy range.

It is not right for every situation, and there are times we would hold off, such as over a fresh injury, an infection, or certain medical conditions. A short conversation before we begin settles whether it is a good fit right now.

What does a session involve?

You talk with your therapist about what is bothering you, then rest comfortably while they work the areas that need it. A session is unhurried, you stay as covered as you like, and the pressure is matched to you: firm where it helps, never a case of gritting your teeth through it. Many people pair massage with their adjustment so the tissue is calmer when the joint is worked.

Massage therapy questions we hear most

Is the massage relaxing or is it therapeutic?

It can be both. The aim is to change how tight tissue moves, and for most people that also feels genuinely relaxing. If you want it lighter or firmer on the day, you tell the therapist and they adjust; it is your session.

Do I need to be a chiropractic patient to book a massage?

No. Many people come in just for massage. That said, when tight muscle is tied to a joint that keeps getting irritated, pairing the two tends to work better than either alone, and we will say so if that looks like your situation.

Does it hurt?

It should not. Deeper work on a stubborn knot can feel intense in a good way, but sharp or bracing pain is not the goal and not necessary. The right pressure is the one you can breathe through, and the therapist works with your feedback.

How often should I come?

It depends on what is going on. An acute, guarded area might benefit from a short run of closer visits; ongoing tension from a desk job might be an occasional tune-up. After your first session the therapist will give you a realistic sense rather than sign you up for a long block.

Who does the massage?

Licensed massage therapists on our staff. They work in the same office as the chiropractors, so your care stays coordinated.
