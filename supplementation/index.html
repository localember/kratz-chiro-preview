What we do

Supplementation in Oregon, WI

If a supplement is a good fit, we will talk it through at your visit in Oregon, alongside your chiropractic care and nutrition guidance. Nothing is required, and nothing is sold as a cure.

The short answer

Supplementation here means a conversation about whether a supplement might support your care, and if so, which one and why. It is offered alongside chiropractic care and nutrition guidance, not as a replacement for either, and not as a treatment for a medical condition. You will never be required to buy a product to be a patient here, and we will be honest when a supplement is not the right next step.

A lot of supplement advice is written to sell a bottle. Here is the straightforward version: what this service is, when it may be worth discussing, and where we will not make claims we cannot stand behind.

What is supplementation?

It is guidance, at your visit, about supplements that may complement how you eat and how you are already being cared for. That can be as simple as reviewing what you already take, or talking through a single, well-chosen option. It is not a pre-packed program, a monthly autoship, or a long list of products to buy on the way out the door.

Food and daily habits come first. A supplement is considered when there is a clear reason to look at one, not as a default add-on to every visit. If we do not think you need one, we will say so.

How it fits with nutrition and chiropractic care

Nutritional counseling and supplementation are related, but they are not the same thing. Nutrition support is about how you eat and the habits you can keep. Supplementation is the narrower question of whether a product might be a useful addition, and only after that conversation. Both sit alongside your chiropractic care. Neither one replaces an adjustment, and neither one is a substitute for advice from your physician or a registered dietitian when that is what your situation needs.

If you already take supplements, bring the bottles or a simple list. That helps us avoid doubling up, and it gives us a clearer picture of what you are already doing.

Who is it for?

It tends to be a useful conversation for people who want a calm, honest answer rather than a sales pitch.

  • Patients already under care who have a question about a supplement they have seen or been offered elsewhere.
  • People who already take several products and want help reviewing the list.
  • Anyone who would rather hear “you do not need that” than be sold something extra.
  • Patients talking through nutrition guidance who want to know whether a supplement belongs in the picture at all.

It is general wellness guidance, not a prescription and not a treatment plan for a diagnosed disease. If your situation belongs with your physician, a specialist, or a registered dietitian, we will say so and point you there.

What does a conversation about supplements look like?

It usually happens during a regular visit, not as a separate appointment you have to book. We ask what you take now, what you are hoping a supplement would help with, and whether that hope is realistic. If something may be a fit, we explain why, what to watch for, and what it is not meant to do. If it is not a fit, the conversation ends there, and your care continues as planned.

We will not promise that a supplement will resolve pain, speed an adjustment, or take the place of care. Those are claims we will not make. A supplement, when it is used, is one small part of a wider picture that still includes your exam, your adjustments, and how you live day to day.

What we will not do

  • Require a supplement purchase as a condition of care.
  • Hand you a stacked protocol of products you did not ask for.
  • Claim that a supplement treats, cures, or prevents a disease.
  • Replace medical care, prescribed medication, or medical nutrition therapy with a bottle.

If a supplement ever seems like a reasonable option, you should still feel free to say no, to check with your physician, or to take time to think about it. That is a normal part of the decision, not a problem.

What does it cost, and do you take insurance?

We have plans available for all kinds of insurance. A conversation about supplementation during your visit does not have a separate required product fee. If you decide to get a supplement, we will be clear about the cost up front.

Supplementation questions we hear most

Do I have to buy supplements to be a patient here?

No. Care is not tied to a product purchase. If a supplement is not a good fit, or you would rather not take one, that is the end of that part of the conversation.

Can I bring the supplements I already take?

Yes, and it helps. Bring the bottles or a written list so we can see what you are already using before anyone suggests something new.

Will a supplement fix my pain?

We would not claim that. A supplement does not replace an exam, an adjustment, or medical care, and we will be straight with you about what it can and cannot do.

Is this the same as nutrition support?

No. Nutrition support is about how you eat and the habits you can keep. Supplementation is only the question of whether a product might be a useful addition, and only when that question is worth asking.

Should I stop a supplement or a medication my doctor recommended?

No. Do not stop or change a prescribed medication, or a supplement your physician asked you to take, without talking to that physician first. We will not ask you to do that.
